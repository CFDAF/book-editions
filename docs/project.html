01The
aim

The question it answers

Search a book and get every edition of that book that exists. That is the user's own specification, and the rest of the project is what it takes to say it honestly.

It grew out of a narrower need: you come across a book and want to know whether an Italian translation exists, from whom and when, before deciding which one to buy. Italy has a good national catalogue, SBN, so that is where most of the engineering went. Italian is shown as one language among the rest all the same, and the lookup works from either end.

Use caseYou typeYou get
UC1English title + authorThe original first edition's title in its original language, then every later edition in every language, newest first, undated last
UC2Italian title + authorThe same result as UC1
UC3Original title + author, in Latin scriptThe same result as UC1
UC4Author onlyEvery book by the author, as works titled in their original language. Picking one reruns as UC3
UC5Title onlyThe one author the sources agree on is adopted and named; if they name several people, a chooser of books and no list

UC1 to UC3 define convergence: the three entry titles of one book must reach the same work, the same original and the same set of editions. No source provides that, so it is a target, and it is measured. Filters for year span, language and publisher apply to the list already in hand and send no request.

Design rule

The header is a publication history, not a verdict. The first version led with “Translated into Italian.” and the user rejected it outright: it answers one narrow question and buries what the editions say.

Completeness has no denominator, because no source can say how many editions exist. So it was made operational, in three parts: reach every record the endpoints hold for the work; precision, meaning no wrong-work record in the default view; and disclosure, saying what is missing and what was refused. Latency comes last and is met by staging: the list first, the expensive work behind it.

02The
sources

The sources: three, four endpoints

Three keyless sources: Wikidata, Open Library and SBN, Italy's national union catalogue. SBN is reached through two unrelated APIs on one host, and they do not return the same fields. Knowing only the first is how the project's founding premise came to be recorded as verified and be wrong (§10).

Each is necessary and none is sufficient. Wikidata's stated original was wrong in 3 of 26 accepted items. Open Library splits translations into separate work records inconsistently, and its first-publish year was wrong on 17 of 60 entries. SBN's work links are partial: over 40 books, 334 same-work records were not linked to their work. The benchmark asked each source whether every entry title of a book reaches the right identity, and every one failed (§05).

Google Books was a fourth source and was removed deliberately: its keyless quota is permanently exhausted and it contributed nothing. The API detail, with every trap in SBN's data, is in docs/sbn-api.md.

03The
shape

The shape

About 10,500 lines of Python: the standard library plus requests. One tree, four packages, and one rule about which of them is allowed to have an opinion.

S0–S4The stages

The work is identified once, in S1, and nothing after it may go back to what the user typed. S2 lists the editions of that identity, and that list is the first thing on the page. S3 and S4 improve it behind the reader. Each stage is walked through in §04.

The old path searched by the typed title, which is why three entry titles of one book gave three different lists and a cold lookup took about a minute. Listing by identity moved the cold median from 34.5 s and 192 requests to 3.5 s and 19.

STOREProducers, one store, a derived view

Each stage writes records with provenance into one store: the source, the route that found the record, and the evidence that admitted it. A record's identity is (source, id), so a row found by two routes is one record with two routes on it. The view is recomputed from the store from scratch after every batch; nothing mutates a display list. Records fold into editions on a shared ISBN and on nothing else, and editions group by language.

APPA job id, then versions

POST /lookup returns a job id at once. The page polls GET /lookup/<id>?v=N and receives either a complete new snapshot or {unchanged: true}. There is no delta and no stream. Each snapshot replaces the last. The command line runs the same job to its end.

python3 server.py                          # the page, on localhost:8000
python3 book_editions.py "Il nome della rosa"
python3 book_editions.py --author "Gregory Bateson"
04The
route

A lookup, stage by stage

There are two front doors and they meet at once. The command line calls app.jobs.run_to_end directly. The browser cannot reach a catalogue itself: SBN sends no Access-Control-Allow-Origin header of any kind, so a static page could never call it. The Python server is forced, not chosen. It puts the page and the data on one origin.

FORKThree questions

A title and an author runs the four stages below. An author with no title is a different question, every book they wrote and not every edition of one, and runs as its own job (see Author mode, further down). A title with no author first asks whose book it is. Wikidata's items, Open Library's work records and up to 100 brief records from SBN's mobile gateway are each tested against the typed title at 0.6 and grouped by person. One person means the lookup runs with that author and the byline says none was typed. Several mean a chooser of books and no list. The OPAC is never asked at this point, because without an author it answers with a different book.

S0Prepare the question

Under a millisecond. The title is validated and, if it is in another script, romanised: the OPAC drops non-Latin free text silently, so nothing non-Latin is ever sent or matched.

S1Identify the work, once · about 2 s

Four answers, and none of them is believed on its own.

Wikidata
The MediaWiki Action API only: the title as a Wikipedia page, whose wikibase_item property gives the item, with a search as the fallback, then one batched wbgetentities call. An item is accepted only when the typed title matches one of the item's own names at 0.6: its labels, its sitelink titles with the disambiguator stripped (1984 (romanzo) → 1984), and its P1476. The rule accepted no wrong item in 91 entries. An item that is itself an edition is not the work.
What it states
P407 and P577, the original language and date. No other free source states them, which is what makes an Italian-first lookup answerable at all. It is also the only step that hands back an author for nothing.
SBN's work
A POST to the OPAC with the title and the author. The answer carries a facet, titolo_uniformef[], listing the uniform titles in the result set. It is read through the A/B guard of §05, never by top count.
Second signal
A work the guard refused or found weak is accepted only if the Wikidata item already accepted names the same work.
Open Library
Searched with the original title, never the typed one. A title test decides which work it is; the edition count only breaks ties, because the count alone picks Animal Farm for 1984.

What the uniform-title facet looks like, asked with a title and an author:

QueryTop facet valueShare

The value is a search key, lowercased and stripped of accents, and it is never shown to the reader. An Italian uniform title for an Italian book is a correct answer meaning not a translation. And a book with no identity is normal: S1 then returns a work that says it found nothing, and the lookup carries on.

S2List the editions of that identity · about 1.5 s

From the identity alone. SBN's records for the work are read a language page at a time, 20 rows to a page, four pages at once; Open Library's editions come by work key. Both run together, on different hosts. No title gate is called here: a record is in the list because a catalogue filed it under this work, which is a statement and not an inference. This is the first snapshot.

S3Recover what the listing cannot reach · about 1.5 s, behind the page

The listing is incomplete by construction, since SBN links only some of a work's records to it. Three routes run beside it: the work's titles as free text, a sweep of the author's name authority, and probes for up to 12 of the ISBNs already in hand. The sweep returns everything the author published, so every row is gated on the work's titles at 0.6, never on what the reader typed. A refused record is kept, with its score, and shown in the loose-match band.

A book with no identity at all gets a different branch: the typed title's route, which served 2 of 96 corpus entries. It is the one place the typed title reaches a gate, and the evidence says so.

S4Read every admitted SBN row in full · behind the page

A listing row is thin, and the fields that tell two printings apart are on the full record only. Each costs one more request.

Up to 1,500 admitted rows are read, four at a time; refused rows are not. Then records fold into editions on a shared ISBN, so one row lists its printing years. A record whose language contradicts itself is disbelieved. Years that are not one plain Gregorian year are checked against SBN's own index. Beside all this, on another host, Open Library is searched for the work's other records, which the reader can add one at a time or all at once. The stage publishes once, at its end. Until then the page says the counts are provisional.

AUTHORAuthor mode

The person is resolved first. SBN's name authority gives one id per person for every Latin form, but a name can answer with several people, and one person can sit under several ids. So the candidates are shown, and the chooser is skipped only when one person answers every form asked, Wikidata's other spellings included. Then the works are listed, one row per work, titled in its original language. SBN caps every facet at 50 items, so the work list is split by language and then by exact year, and what no split covers is a stated count. What the person only edited, prefaced or is the subject of goes in a separate group, closed by default. Clicking a work reruns it as a title lookup under every spelling of its title.

05Identity

The identity problem

Noise, Rumori and Bruits are one book. Their titles share no word. Everything hard in this project is some form of that sentence.

Title similarity across languages is exactly 0.0. Of 608 same-work rows measured, 580 score exactly 0.0 or 1.0 against the work's titles. A title test therefore cannot tell whether a record is the right book in general. It can only tell whether the work has a title in the record's language. So a threshold is not the lever, and something has to state that two records are one work.

SourceBooks where every entry reaches the right identityOf 91 entries
SBN's uniform title22 / 37right on 68, wrong on 0, none on 23
Wikidata14 / 37labels and sitelinks of the work item
Open Library13 / 37one work key, when translations are not split

That is the benchmark's first assumption, and it failed in every source. The failure is a constraint, and the design follows from it: a book with no identity is normal, and the page has to say so.

GUARDWhich SBN work, and may it be believed

SBN files editions against a uniform-title authority. Asked with a title and an author, the OPAC returns the uniform titles in the result set. The work guard scores two things. A is the typed title against the uniform title. B is, of the query's records really carrying that title, the share SBN files under the work.

VerdictRuleWhat sits there
StrongA ≥ 0.6, or B > 0.5Accepted
WeakB = 0.5The known wrong works: Tesi di filosofia della storia, Angelus Novus
RefusedotherwiseClassics SBN links thinly: Crime and Punishment 9/31, Der Process 2/15, Blindness 3/8, all at B ≤ 0.375

The wrong works sit above every refused classic, so loosening B to rescue the classics admits the wrong works first. Over 91 entries the guard accepted no wrong work. The way in is a second, independent signal: a refused or weak work is accepted when the Wikidata item accepted for the same entry has a label, sitelink or title matching it at 0.6 or more. That rescued 7 of 19 refused-or-weak entries, with 0 agreements in 2,024 cross-book pairs.

GATEDoes this record belong to the work

A record is reported only if something ties it to the work: the work authority, a shared ISBN, or a title match at IDENTIFYING_TITLE_MATCH = 0.6 against one of the work's titles. It was once 0.45, where one shared word passed. Two cases pin it.

The two that must stay out

L'ordine delle notizie scores 0.5 against the titles of The Invention of News. Quale socialismo, quale Europa is by Attali, from 1977, like Bruits, and scores 0.0: same author and same era is not enough. A number is also not the only way to move this gate. The romanisation, the transliteration fold and the stopword list change what scores 0.6 without touching it, so they carry the same re-check.

The score is not an edit distance. Each title becomes a set of tokens, with accents stripped, stopwords dropped and transliteration variants folded, and the score is the overlap over the longer title: len(A & B) / max(len(A), len(B)).

What the gate refuses is not thrown away. It is shown in a collapsed loose-match band, each row labelled with how it was reached, because the gate is most likely wrong exactly where the score is 0.0.

06The
answer

What comes back

A snapshot: everything the page shows and nothing it does not. It holds the header, the editions by language, the language order, the publisher facets, the choices when several books share a title, the coverage ledger, the duplicate hints, the loose matches and Open Library's other records. Around it the job reports its status, its stage, each source's state, the requests made and failed, and whether grouping is still running. The command line formats the same snapshot as text, JSON or CSV.

Filters never touch any of it. The header, the spans and the counts are computed over the whole store, so changing a filter re-filters the rows in the browser, sends no request and moves no number in the header.

PAGEThe page, top to bottom

  • The search. A title, an author, and two switches that are off by default: other spellings of the title, other spellings of the author.
  • The publication history. The work's title; the original, where a source states one, with who stated it; the earliest edition found, always; then one row per language with its year span and edition count.
  • The coverage ledger. One line, 192 records found for this work, that opens into how they were reached: 3 linked to this work in SBN, 7 in Open Library's record for this work. Under it, in red, any source that came back short and any list that was read only in part.
  • Filters, as chips. Language, publisher and a year span, multi-select. A row with no language is a chip like any other.
  • The editions, grouped by language, newest first. Each row leads with year and publisher, the two fields that tell editions of one book apart, and carries what the catalogue says about it: its medium, credited to X, a label on a year that had to be settled.
  • A bracket between two rows from different sources that may be the same edition. It is a hint and never a merge.
  • A row, opened: the translator, the physical description, the libraries holding a copy, and where to buy one.
  • A note under the list that SBN links study guides, graded readers, omnibus volumes and adaptations to a work, and that they are labelled only where the catalogue's own fields say so.
  • Open Library's other records for the work, to add one at a time or all at once. They stay out of the header.
  • The loose-match band, collapsed: every record the title gate refused, labelled with how it was reached.

PHRASINGSentences the header refuses to write

The origin line is composed once, in core/view.py, so the page and the command line cannot word one claim two ways.

A source statesThe header says
year and languagefirst published 1972 in inglese
year onlyfirst published 1972
language onlyoriginally in inglese, earliest edition found 1976
neitherNo source states when this work first appeared.

A known original language is never paired with a fallback year: first published 1976 in inglese would take the year from an Italian edition and the language from the English original, and be false. The original and the earliest edition found are stated side by side and never compared. Editions dated before a stated original are left out of the earliest line. And a language's row is marked as the original only when the row can support it: the stated original year has to fall within the years that row spans.

COPIESWhere to get one

Buy links are search URLs built from the row's ISBN, with no network, no price and no stock: IBS, Amazon.it, Feltrinelli and Mondadori new; AbeBooks and Maremagnum used. A row with no ISBN gets no links, because a title search lands on whichever edition the shop sells now and not the one the reader opened. For those, and for anything out of print, the answer is SBN's holdings list, or the Internet Archive where Open Library records a scan. Libraccio has no link at all: its search is a POST form.

08The
decisions

The decisions that mattered

The log holds 56 lettered decisions, A to BD, each with the number that decided it and what was discarded. The code cites them by letter. These twelve shaped the product; the letters are the log's own.

A
A second acceptance signal for a refused SBN work, instead of a looser guard.

Rescues 7 of 19 refused-or-weak entries; 0 agreements in 2,024 cross-book pairs. Re-measured after the fold: 11 of 11.

B
Latin script only; other scripts are romanised before matching or sending.

The OPAC silently drops non-Latin free text and answers with the unfiltered set.

D
No “first edition differs” inference. The header shows the stated original and, always, the earliest edition found.

The inference raised 8 false flags and 1 miss over 36 books. The rule that replaced it was older than the true first edition 0 times in 37.

F
A recovery route beside the listing, title-gated.

The listing alone drops 334 SBN records and 98 Open Library editions. The routes that find them leaked 44 wrong-work records when ungated.

H
A cross-source duplicate is a hint, never a merge. A bracket joins the two rows; only a shared ISBN folds them.

Hint precision 0.766 on 100 hand-judged pairs, 0.866 after page counts were allowed to withhold one (AX).

I
One edition row per ISBN, listing its printing years, grouped in the background.

483 records with an ISBN become 297 rows. Grouping from listing fields instead had a precision of 0.425.

M
A job id and polling, with the first snapshot being S2's complete list (AA).

Discarded: streaming, one blocking response, and a header-only first version that read 0 editions for a second and a half.

N
Two disclosures: the coverage ledger and the loose-match band.

Measured per-lookup numbers on one line, and everything the title gate refused, collapsed. They replaced a fixed note that said the same thing on every lookup.

Z
A non-edition is labelled, never filtered.

SBN links study guides, retellings, omnibus volumes and films to a work. A row says what the catalogue says: its medium, and credited to X where the heading is another person.

AH
The Dewey routes are deleted.

On full records the branch fires on 323 refused records and would admit 1, an omnibus. With SBN's own Dewey, 3 of the 4 it admits are other books.

AN
Title only: adopt the one author the sources agree on, and say so.

If Wikidata, Open Library and SBN's catalogued titles name several people, a chooser of books and no list. This restored the use case dropped on day three.

AY
A year in another calendar is settled by two readings or not at all, and the row says which.

65 of 156 Persian records showed their Iranian year. The bracketed year and SBN's own index were each wrong once, so neither is trusted alone.

One decision is about deleting code rather than writing it, and it is the reason the release is one tree: no path is deleted while it reaches a correct edition or original the other does not (AM). The full list is in docs/DECISIONS.md §3.

10The
traps

The traps

One shape repeats in nearly all of them: the catalogue answers confidently and wrongly instead of failing. Nothing returns an error. You get a plausible list that is not what you asked for.

09-12How a verified fact turned out to be wrong

For its first day the project stood on one sentence, recorded as checked: no free catalogue records the link between a translation and its original. Two of the findings behind it failed the same way.

The first: Open Library has no translation_of field. It does. The record that was checked, /works/OL15764295W.json for Rumori, is a work record, and translation_of and translated_from live on editions. Kafka a la platja carries translation_of: 海辺のカフカ.

The second, and the one that mattered: SBN records carry no uniform or original title. True of the mobile gateway, false of SBN. The catalogue records the link as a UNIMARC uniform-title authority, shown in its OPAC as Titolo di opera. That field is simply absent from the mobile gateway's JSON, so six full records showed no trace of it, and the conclusion was drawn from a hole in one API.

It was found by chasing one book that nothing could reach. A lookup for More Brilliant Than the Sun found three English editions and stopped, while SBN was holding the Italian translation, Più brillante del sole, and its own statement that the two are one work. The Italian record shares no ISBN with the English editions, scores 0.0 on title similarity and carries no Dewey class. The author sweep was already finding it and then discarding it for lack of any signal. Pursuing that one failure, on the evening of 2026-09-12, surfaced the OPAC's own JSON API and its titolo_uniformef[] facet, which names the work a title belongs to; filtering the same search by that value returns every record linked to it. Two requests of about 0.3 s each.

The premise had not been idle. The Dewey class as a bridge, the scoring around it and the author sweep with its gate all existed to work around a gap that was only ever a gap in one JSON view. The other bridges infer that two records describe one work; this one reads SBN saying so. It went in as a fourth bridge that same day (commit e871e724), and the rebuild later made it the spine of the lookup.

The standing rule

Check the record type before concluding a field is absent. In both cases a field was declared missing after looking at the wrong kind of object: a work record instead of an edition record, one API instead of the catalogue. Six negatives read as proof.

Unknown parameter, unfiltered answer

Both SBN APIs ignore a parameter they do not know and return the unfiltered set. There is one whitelist per API, and the OPAC's refuses a key outside it instead of filtering.

Non-Latin text, dropped

The OPAC silently drops non-Latin free text. Alone, it returns all 21,806,046 records; with an author, Доктор Живаго returns the author's whole catalogue, 855 = 855. Latin script is sent, and each new key or value class ships with a bogus-value control that must return 0.

A work without an author

The OPAC answers, and the answer is a different book. Bare Rumori returns Russolo's arte dei rumori; bare The Essential Knuth returns essential mathematics for economic analysis. The functions raise instead of querying.

The language field lies

linguaPubblicazione is the only language signal, and it sometimes names the language translated from. UBO4636099, Vintage's English Kafka on the Shore, is filed GIAPPONESE. A record naming a translator while claiming the original language is disbelieved, not corrected. paesePubblicazione is a country and says nothing about language.

A 200 that is not an answer

A single / in an SBN free-text value returns HTTP 200 and a Solr error object. Being valid JSON, it was cached and replayed for 24 hours, so every repeat read SBN partial. The cache now refuses what its caller rejects.

Throttled without a contact

Wikimedia rate-limits by User-Agent: 102 of 180 requests refused without a contact URL, 0 of 180 with one. It had been recorded as a connect stall to one Wikipedia host. It was 35% of request time.

Facets that count the wrong thing

The mobile gateway returns facet counts and accepts none of them back as filters: lingua=ita, fq=, facet=, every form is ignored. The OPAC's facets do filter. And SBN's lingua facet counts the author's catalogue, not the work: francese 124 beside one Italian edition. Language chips are counted from the rows on the page.

The data is dirty in specific ways

Non-sorting articles arrive wrapped in the control characters \x88…\x89. Several Dewey codes run together with no separator. In holdings, the library name is in shelfmark and the city in invNum. The first line of a listing row is not the imprint on 26 of 2,603 rows.

The stale server

Stop the server with pkill -f server.py. macOS reports the process as Python server.py, so pkill -f "python3 server.py" matches nothing and a stale instance keeps serving the code it started with. Started as python3 -m app.server, it needs pkill -f 'app.server'. Same shape again: the wrong command reports success.

Smaller ones, each paid for once: a uniform-title label is a lowercased, accent-stripped search key and is never displayed. A language code is read off SBN's facet and never guessed from the Italian name: SLOVACCO is slo. And a record count is never asserted, because Nineteen Eighty-Four returned 733 records and then 899 on the same 35 requests hours later.

11The
ledger

Failure honesty: the ledger

A failed request must never be indistinguishable from an empty result. That it once was is the worst bug the project had.

Every helper used to return “no results” when a request failed. Editions went silently missing while each source read ok. For a tool whose answer is sometimes there is no Italian edition, that is the one failure that cannot be tolerated: absence and breakage have to look different.

So every call goes on a ledger, one per lookup, opened by the job in its own thread. It counts a failure whether or not the source that swallowed it says so. What the reader sees are readings of it:

  • partial (N failed) beside a source, counting every failed request;
  • an Incomplete note saying editions may be missing;
  • a slow-lookup note above about 10 seconds, naming only causes measured in that lookup;
  • a line for every cap that bound, because every truncation is on the page.

All of them are worded in one module, core/notes.py, so the page, the command line and a test cannot say different things about the same lookup.

The pool that drops it

A plain thread pool does not carry the ledger into its workers, so their failures would reach no reading at all. Every pool in the project is the project's own Pool, and a test scans the four packages for the other kind by text.

BELOWWhat the network layer guarantees

Timeouts
(4, 15) seconds, connect and read apart, so a stalled connection fails fast
Retries
2, backoff 0.3, on 429, 500, 502, 503 and 504, for GET and POST alike: a search has no side effect. A 404 is not retried
Sessions
one requests.Session per thread
User-Agent
names the client and carries a contact URL, in the form Wikimedia's policy asks for
Per call
the ledger records the source and host; whether the network, the cache or the test fixtures answered; how long it took; its error; and each retry with its cause and any Retry-After wait
Validation
a caller passes the check only it can make, so a 200 carrying an error object is a failure and is never cached

The same principle runs through the rest. A Retry-After over 60 seconds is a failure, not a wait. An offline run that cannot answer a request stops loudly; it never degrades to “no results”. And where a source states no original, the header shows none: uncertainty shows less.

Why lowering a cap hides records

Every cap is reported on the page when it binds. Lowering one to go faster does not fix anything; it removes records and then has to say so. The speed came from staging, not from budgets: the list first, the full records behind it.

CACHEWhy it is load-bearing

A cold lookup is dozens to hundreds of requests. Each response is written to .cache/ as JSON under a hash of its URL and parameters and kept for 24 hours, so a repeat is instant. Without it the tool is unusable to iterate on and impolite to public catalogues. Delete .cache/ to force fresh data.

The cache refuses what its caller rejects. Both SBN APIs answer a request they dislike with HTTP 200 and a valid JSON body saying so, which is exactly what a cache stores. Such a body is never written, and one already on disk is dropped on sight.

13Proof

How it is verified

Four different things, and none of them substitutes for another.

1An offline suite

python3 -m pytest runs 1,170 tests in about two seconds. It is offline by construction: the test session blocks socket.connect, and recorded catalogue bodies are replayed under the HTTP layer, where every source has to pass. core/, where every gate lives, is held at 100% of executable lines by a standard-library tracer. The stages are tested with the catalogues stubbed and the server over a fake connection, never a port.

Every literal in the parser tests came from a real recorded body, because the traps are counter-intuitive and an invented example tests the wrong thing.

2A control for every fact

Since both SBN APIs answer nonsense with the unfiltered set, a filter that seems to work proves nothing. Each of the 20 verified facts was checked with a control: a bogus value that must return 0. A fact resting on one facet value has a shelf life. One of them was re-measured when SBN re-filed its example.

3A corpus, and six books kept out of it

40 books with up to three entry titles each (original, English, Italian), 16 hand-written regression cases, and 9 people for author mode. Six books were held out of tuning and checked only at gates. On them, cold, over 96 entries: a strong SBN work on 0.765 against 0.811 on the fitting set, and an original stated and wrong 0 times on both.

What that check cannot say

The build does not do worse on books it was not tuned on. No more than that: the corpus is literary, canonical and heavily Italian-published, the six were chosen after the first decisions, and refusal behaviour was never held out.

4A real port

627 green tests and a dead sweep

Because catalog/ may not import core/, its parsers hand back plain dicts. A record whose holdings were still dicts raised only where the view read one, which no tested row did. Green is not “the pipeline runs”. That takes a real server and a cold lookup, and one property of the page (a filter change issues no request) can only be checked in a browser.

14Limits

Limits and open items

What the shipped lookup still cannot do. The page says most of these itself when they apply.

  • No stated original, no original. The header states one only where Wikidata or SBN's work authority does. The earliest edition found is always shown, and it is a statement about the catalogues, not about history.
  • Retranslations filed under a separate work are not joined. Sul concetto di storia is its own uniform title, and nothing ties it to Tesi di filosofia della storia.
  • A same-work record in a language the work has no title in is refused, and lands in the loose-match band. Adaptations sharing the work's title are admitted, labelled.
  • The duplicate hint is a mark, never a merge. It was right on 0.866 of the hand-judged pairs.
  • Open Library's main work is sometimes the wrong record: 8 entries in the corpus, where the right one is not on the duplicate list either.
  • Author mode reads 500 records under a name for the group filed under no work, says so when it stops, and lets the reader read the rest.
  • Cold latency has a long tail: p90 19.1 s and a 95.3 s maximum to the end of S4, though the list is on the page long before.
  • Buy links are search URLs, not live stock or price.
